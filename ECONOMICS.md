# Venture-Economics Snapshot

> Pre-test model dated September 15, 2026. Dollar amounts below are assumptions, not observed results.

| Element | Assumption | Logic |
|---|---:|---|
| User, buyer, and payer | Undergraduate student taking 4+ courses | The same student experiences the pain, chooses the product, and pays. |
| Price | $4.99 per semester | A small, one-time semester purchase aligned with the syllabus-entry buying moment. |
| Payment rhythm | Up to twice per academic year | A student would purchase when organizing each fall or spring term. |
| Payment processing | $0.45 per purchase | Planning estimate: 2.9% of $4.99 plus $0.30, rounded up. |
| Hosting/processing | $0.05 per purchase | Planning allowance for static hosting and light client-side processing. |
| Total variable cost | $0.50 per purchase | $0.45 + $0.05. |
| Contribution | $4.49 per purchase | $4.99 revenue − $0.50 variable cost. |
| Contribution margin | 90.0% | $4.49 ÷ $4.99. |

## Acquisition and cash implication

The first acquisition path is direct access to students through course group chats, campus organizations, residence halls, and peer referrals. Our pre-test ceiling is **$2.00 in acquisition cost per first purchase**, leaving **$2.49 after variable and acquisition costs** ($4.99 − $0.50 − $2.00).

At 100 paid semester purchases, this model implies:

- Revenue: **$499**
- Variable costs: **$50**
- Contribution before acquisition: **$449**
- Maximum planned acquisition spend: **$200**
- Cash remaining after variable cost and planned acquisition: **$249**, before fixed costs and team labor

## Repeat purchase and fragile assumptions

We assume **50% of paying users purchase again the following semester**. This has not been tested. The most fragile economic assumption is whether enough students value avoided manual entry enough to pay $4.99 after trying the workflow. The paid-offer click tests interest, not a completed purchase, so it is weaker evidence than collected revenue.

## Evidence connection

In the completed MVP test, **15 of 20 eligible participants (75%) clicked the $4.99-per-semester offer** after using the product. This supports testing $4.99 in a real payment experiment rather than lowering the price based on opinions alone. It does **not** establish a 75% purchase-conversion rate because the MVP collected neither payment nor contact information. Actual checkout completion, acquisition cost, repeat purchase, and support costs remain unresolved economic assumptions.
