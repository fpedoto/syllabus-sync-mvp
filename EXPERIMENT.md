# Venture Experiment

Venture: SyllabusSync
Experiment: Calendar Conversion Test
Team members: Francie Pedoto and Grace Pavloff
Precommit date: September 15, 2026
Planned testing period: September 15-19, 2026

## Causal claim

We believe undergraduate students currently managing at least four courses experiences time-consuming manual calendar entry and the risk of missing assignments or exams because
important deadlines are scattered across syllabi. If offered a tool that converts real syllabus text into a reviewed, downloadable calendar file, we expect
students to complete an unassisted calendar download within 5 minutes and consider a $4.99-per-semester offer because the product reduces manual work and makes important deadlines easier to track.

## Precommitted decision rule

- Target segment: Undergraduate students currently enrolled in at least four courses who have access to a real current or recent syllabus containing deadlines. SyllabusSync team members are not eligible.
- Test action: Using text from one real syllabus, the participant must identify deadlines, review the results, and download an .ics calendar file without procedural help.
- Planned denominator: 20 eligible undergraduate students invited to complete the test. Eligible invitees who decline, do not open the MVP, or abandon the process will remain in the denominator.
- Threshold written before data: Continue only if at least 8 of 20 eligible invitees complete an unassisted calendar download within five minutes and at least 4 of 20 click the $4.99 offer.
- If the result is below the threshold, we will: Change the workflow, value proposition, or pricing if 4–7 of 20 complete the primary action, or if at least 8 complete it but fewer than 4 click the paid offer. We will stop or pivot this version if fewer than 4 of 20 complete the primary action.
- If the result meets the threshold, we will: Continue developing SyllabusSync and design a subsequent experiment testing calendar accuracy and actual willingness to pay.

## Ethics and consent

- What the participant is told before acting: This is an early prototype created for a student class project. Participation is voluntary and may be stopped at any time. The team is evaluating the product, not the participant. No payment will be collected. Participants should not submit grades, student identification numbers, private instructor communications, or other sensitive personal information.
- Data collected: Anonymous participant identifier, eligibility status, whether each product step was completed, whether assistance was required, completion or abandonment time, whether the calendar opened successfully, whether the $4.99 offer was clicked, and anonymized observations about hesitation or confusion.
- Data deliberately not collected: Names, email addresses, student identification numbers, grades, account credentials, original syllabus text, private instructor communications, screenshots, audio recordings, video recordings, or payment information.
- Retention/deletion plan: The team will retain only anonymized counts and behavioral notes until the course grade and any related review period are complete. Any temporary testing notes containing identifying information will be deleted immediately after anonymization and no later than September 20, 2026. The MVP will process syllabus text locally in the participant’s browser and will not intentionally transmit or permanently store it.
- Why this test creates no unreasonable deception, pressure, or privacy risk: Participants are told that the product is an early class-project prototype and that the paid offer measures interest rather than collecting payment. Participation is voluntary, no academic or financial benefit depends on participating, participants may stop at any time, and the test avoids collecting sensitive information.
